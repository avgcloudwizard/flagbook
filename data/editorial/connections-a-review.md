## Additional candidates and exclusions

Investigated ten topics for nine slots. Ketchup’s journey was the additional candidate: [Etymonline’s full entry](https://www.etymonline.com/word/ketchup) was read and presents competing etymological possibilities. A confident single-origin claim was excluded rather than converted into a catchy certainty. This does not mean no careful ketchup fact could ever be written.

The user’s Madagascar [PMC starting URL](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3223632/) returned a browser challenge. The linked topic was verified through the readable PLOS article instead. A later [Nature/Scientific Reports paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26066) and its PMC mirror also failed to open, so their search snippets were not used as final evidence. The original paper’s more specific settlement hypotheses were deliberately not repeated as established fact.

A historic Council of Europe PDF redirected to a general page, and a Norwegian museum page returned 403. Neither is cited in the final records. The Tomigusuku city PDF was discovered as corroboration for Brazil but did not return readable text in the browser; it is not used in the published record.

Map points indicate ports where the sources name them, or clearly marked approximate regions. Connecting lines describe the relationship, not an exact ship, train or ancient migration route. Country codes are contemporary browsing tags rather than historical citizenship assertions.
